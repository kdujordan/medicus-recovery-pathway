The gap after discharge

Too many patients return to hospital. Usually not because care failed.

description

Instructions misunderstood

A discharge plan explained once, at a busy bedside, is hard to follow alone at home.

medication

Medications missed

A skipped antibiotic dose or a stopped painkiller goes unnoticed until the patient is back on the ward.

insights

Warning signs unseen

Rising pain, fever or a worrying wound often show days earlier. Nobody is looking at the right moment.

Medicus keeps watching after the patient leaves. The care team sees what changes, and acts on it.

How it works

One pathway from the ward to full recovery

Each step follows the patient's own discharge plan, written and approved by their clinicians.

Before discharge

Enrol and teach back

Staff enrol the patient in under 10 minutes, record consent, language and a caregiver, and confirm the patient can explain today's tasks and urgent symptoms.

Teach-back done
Every day

A short daily check-in

Pain, medicines, wound, appetite and how they feel, in about 3 minutes. Photos of the wound go to a clinician for review.

About 3 minutes
Continuously

Optional monitoring bracelet

For higher-risk patients, a bracelet tracks pulse, movement and skin-temperature trend, and reports how reliable each reading is. A thermometer confirms a fever.

High-risk kit
Within minutes

Rules flag, AI prioritises

Clinician-written rules catch red flags and missed check-ins. AI summarises the trend and puts the patients who need attention first at the top of the list.

UrgentReview
Until closed

A nurse acts and closes the loop

Every alert has an owner and a response deadline. The nurse calls, records what was done and the outcome, and escalates when needed.
